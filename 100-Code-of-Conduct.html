# 100 - Code of Conduct & Workplace Policies


## 1.0 Purpose

Innovatech Solutions is committed to fostering a professional, respectful, and inclusive work environment. This policy outlines the standards of conduct expected from all employees, contractors, and temporary staff.


## 2.0 Scope

This policy applies to all individuals performing work for or on behalf of Innovatech Solutions, both in the office and in remote work locations. It also covers conduct at company-sponsored events, including travel and social gatherings.


## 3.0 Core Principles


### 3.1 Professionalism and Respect

All employees must treat colleagues, clients, partners, and vendors with dignity and respect. Harassment, discrimination, or bullying of any kind will not be tolerated.


### 3.2 Integrity and Ethics

Employees are expected to act with honesty and integrity. This includes avoiding conflicts of interest (both actual and perceived), protecting company assets, and complying with all applicable laws and regulations.


### 3.3 Anti-Harassment and Non-Discrimination

Innovatech is an equal opportunity employer. We prohibit discrimination and harassment based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, sexual orientation, gender identity, or any other protected status.


- **Harassment** includes unwelcome conduct that is based on a protected characteristic and creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment.

- **Reporting:** Any employee who experiences or witnesses harassment or discrimination should report it immediately to their manager, the P&C team, or the anonymous Ethics Hotline. Employees are not required to report concerns to the person accused of misconduct. Innovatech prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.

- **Triage:** Reports and concerns raised under this policy will be triaged under Section 4.0.


### 3.4 Confidentiality

Employees must protect the confidentiality of company trade secrets, client information, and other proprietary data. This obligation continues even after employment ends. Refer to the **Data Security Policy (600)** for specific handling requirements.


### 3.5 Substance Abuse

The use, possession, or distribution of illegal drugs or alcohol while on company premises, while performing company business, or while operating company vehicles is strictly prohibited. Being under the influence of such substances during work hours is also prohibited.


### 3.6 Workplace Violence

Innovatech has a zero-tolerance policy for violence, threats of violence, and intimidation in the workplace. If there is an immediate danger, contact emergency services first. Any such behavior should then be reported immediately to the Security Team or P&C.


## 4.0 Triage of Reported Concerns


### 4.1 Purpose

Innovatech will triage reported concerns in a consistent, timely, and documented manner. Triage determines what process a concern needs, how urgently it should be handled, and what interim steps may be necessary. Triage does not decide guilt, credibility, or discipline.


### 4.2 Decision Owner and Timing

A trained P&C/Employee Relations triage specialist, or a P&C designee, will triage concerns. The triage decision should ordinarily be made within 24 to 48 hours of intake. Immediate safety or emergency issues must be addressed first.


The triage decision owner must not be the person accused or otherwise conflicted. If objectivity is compromised—for example, because the accused is a senior executive—the concern must be escalated to an alternative P&C contact, a senior leader, the Ethics Hotline, or another designated reporting channel.


### 4.3 Step 1 — Clarify the Core Issue

Before classifying the concern, triage will include a brief, neutral intake conversation or review of the report. Open-ended questions should be used to distinguish a management-style or interpersonal issue from a potential protected-class, retaliation, safety, or other legally significant issue.


Where appropriate, triage should ask whether the employee believes the conduct is connected to a protected characteristic, protected activity, leave, accommodation request, or other legally protected circumstance.


The intake record should capture the employee’s exact words and the answers given.


### 4.4 Step 2 — Classify the HR Domain

Triage will classify the concern against relevant HR domains, which may include:


- performance management;

- discipline;

- harassment;

- discrimination;

- retaliation;

- leave or accommodation;

- workplace violence or threats;

- bullying or abusive behavior;

- conflicts of interest;

- confidentiality or data security;

- substance abuse;

- other workplace conduct concerns.


### 4.5 Step 3 — Assign the Response Level

Priority is separate from response level. Response level determines the process required.


| Level | Process Required | When It Applies |

|---|---|---|

| **Level 1** | Supervisor handling with documentation | Minor interpersonal friction with no legal floor |

| **Level 2** | P&C review | Ambiguous complaint needing clarification |

| **Level 3** | Formal internal investigation: plan, interviews, findings | Allegations of harassment, discrimination, retaliation, safety, wage issues, or other matters with a legal floor |

| **Level 4** | Outside investigator | Internal objectivity is compromised, such as when the accused is an executive or the internal function is conflicted |

| **Level 5** | Counsel involvement and immediate protective steps | Severe legal exposure or urgent safety risk |


Where a legal floor applies—for example, allegations involving protected characteristics, protected leave, accommodation, retaliation, safety, or wage issues—the case cannot be resolved through coaching alone. A **Level 3 formal internal investigation is the minimum required response**, unless facts require Level 4 or Level 5.


### 4.6 Step 4 — Assign Priority and Service-Level Targets

Priority is driven by impact, regulatory risk, safety, and the population affected.


| Priority | Trigger Conditions | Default Targets |

|---|---|---|

| **P1 — Critical** | Regulatory deadline within 48 hours; safety or welfare risk; pay impact in the current cycle; multi-employee or executive impact | Response: 1 business hour; Resolution: 8 business hours |

| **P2 — High** | Time-bound impact within 5 business days; single-employee material impact | Response: 4 business hours; Resolution: 2 business days |

| **P3 — Standard** | Routine inquiry with no time pressure | Response: 1 business day; Resolution: 3 business days |

| **P4 — Low** | Informational, bulk, or non-time-sensitive matter | Response: 2 business days; Resolution: 5 business days |


These are internal service-level targets and may be adjusted based on facts, legal requirements, and available resources. A case should be escalated—for example, from P2 to P1—if interim steps reveal immediate retaliation, safety risk, or other urgent harm.


### 4.7 Step 5 — Define Immediate Next Actions

The triage output must include the immediate actions required. Depending on the concern, these may include:


1. Documenting the complaint as reported, including the employee’s exact words and intake answers.

2. Preserving relevant evidence, including suspending auto-deletion for relevant email, chat, scheduling, or performance records where appropriate.

3. Assessing interim protective steps, such as temporary reporting-line or scheduling adjustments, without shifting the burden to the complainant.

4. Assigning an appropriate investigator or response owner and opening a case file.

5. Avoiding informal “quiet words” with the accused or the accused’s supervisor before an investigation plan exists where doing so could contaminate evidence or create retaliation risk.


### 4.8 Triage Output and Records

The triage record should include, at minimum:


| Triage Element | Required Entry |

|---|---|

| Core issue | Brief description of the concern |

| HR domains | Relevant domains identified |

| Response level | Level 1–5 |

| Priority | P1–P4 |

| First actions | Immediate steps required |

| Decision owner | Person responsible for triage |

| Decision deadline | Date and time for triage completion |


Triage records will be handled as confidentially as practical, consistent with Section 3.4 and applicable law.


### 4.9 Triage Principle: Triage the Content, Not the Delivery

A quiet, hedged, or informally worded complaint can carry the same legal weight as a loud or formal one. Triage must focus on the content of the concern, not the employee’s tone, confidence, or delivery. Downgrading a concern based on tone is a common and costly triage error.


### 4.10 Relationship to Investigations and Discipline

Triage determines the process, priority, and interim steps; it does not determine the outcome. Innovatech may investigate reported concerns. Employees are expected to cooperate honestly and preserve relevant information. Investigations are handled as confidentially as practical, but confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Outcomes depend on facts, evidence, policy, and applicable law, and are not guaranteed to end in discipline.


## 5.0 Disciplinary Action

Violations of this Code of Conduct may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment. The specific action will depend on the severity and nature of the violation. Triage under Section 4.0 does not determine discipline; any disciplinary action will be consistent with applicable law and the circumstances.

- **Healthcare Benefits Policy (510), Section 16.0** — nondiscrimination in benefits.

- **Travel & Entertainment Policy (900)** — conduct during company travel and sponsored events.
